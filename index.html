RESEARCH PROJECT 02 / MULTILINGUAL CORPUS

Red in three versions of Red Sorghum.

I compared red-related words in the Chinese novel and its English and Spanish translations. The site shows what the corpus measures—word frequencies, nearby words and sentence-level similarity—without treating those patterns as proof of a cultural shift.

ANALYSIS STEPS

From text files to comparable summaries.

I checked file encoding and text boundaries, kept source offsets in the sentence records, and counted nearby words in sentence-bounded ±5-token windows. I then compared multilingual sentence embeddings for sentences containing candidate forms with length-matched control sentences.

The candidate lists define what this analysis searches for; they are not exact translation equivalents. The versions share a five-part structure but are not aligned sentence by sentence, so the results describe patterns within this novel rather than translation effects in general.

INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS

Associations are not interpretations by themselves.

The word associations differ across versions, and candidate contexts show a small increase in similarity relative to matched controls. Neither result establishes the direction or cultural meaning of a translation shift. That would require aligned passages, human annotation and tests with alternative candidate lists.

The site shares aggregate counts and scores only. It does not distribute novel text, sentence excerpts or embeddings that could recover text.
